title: Notes on the children’s corridor

Unpacking M. Montessori’s prepared environment: let us align on “place as teacher.” Cf also Reggio Emilia  

The conventional approach to education (it reaches its apotheosis with Loom’s XP Arcade/Bling School model) narrows the experience of learning

Too much emphasis on “instruction” (content, etc.) constricts it tightly; focus on extrinsic motivation drains learning of its potential for joy, absorption

Only an anemic kind of learning is possible then indeed

Instruction can be useful in some moments, e.g., when the learner has a living, urgent question and seeks out specific distinctions from an authority, someone with chops / good sense / fellow-feeling. 

But we underestimate the full blooded learning with which a learner is continuously engaged by being inside a Place

- The learner drinks in: values, rhythms, textures, tonalities, belief systems/assumptions, intrapersonal/interpersonal/interspecies norms
    
- It’s always happening anyway — regardless of how intentionally the Place has been designed
    
Therefore designing or finding or funding appropriate Places where learning can happen is a most high leverage enterprise for the aspiring educator (cf. Chet Bowers, Francesco Tonucci) 

Therefore also providing age-appropriate scaffolding for places that are misaligned with a child’s developmental needs (cf. Fone especially) also a most high-leverage activity.
