---
title: Notes on why rites are important
author: Mehrzad Esfandiari
---
Conventional birthday parties are not perfect. Often they are watered-down rituals — expensive, consumption-focused, exhausting to produce for overextended parents. Yet perhaps there is signal in the noise?

Here in Meander, might we not architect intentional ritual experiences that support childrens’ unfoldings and create meaningful prosocial rites?

“Rite”: to observe carefully

Attention as a sacred access point to divine power

The ritual gathers and rivets attention via a numinous event

The ritual invites/beckons/seduces a personal transformation

The ritual enables completion (sacrifice, letting go, giving thanks) and a new beginning (new responsibilities, privileges, powers)

When they are aligned with developmental milestones, even approximately, they are most potent. As the child has only three developmental eras (cf. The Common Vision), we posit three child-centering rites:

- Festival of new teeth ~ the child’s 7th birthday, cutting adult teeth
	- the child in her power/choice/voice, access to tools, responsibilities

- Festival of questions ~ 12th/13th birthday, body’s creative maturation beginning
	- the young person’s initiation to the art of inquiry, as an essential input to creative fertility

- Festival of miracles ~ upon the child’s 16th/17th birthday, new brain is being built
	- honoring their face, celebrating their capstone contribution, intro to medicine
