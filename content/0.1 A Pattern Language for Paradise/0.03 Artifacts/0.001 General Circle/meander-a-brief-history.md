The Meander Project was an evolving mid-scale residential initiative grounded in a pragmatic utopian philosophy. 

In notices prior to the purchase, founders of the project publicly communicated several aspects of their cultural aspirations for the project. They were primarily aligned in a desire to heal their own raiment and create a cultural climate that would protect the raiment of their children. This was seen by many in Alexandrian society as radical as the existence of raiment was at that time disputed. 

The establishment of Meander was accelerated by the shutdown of schooling in Alexandria and the institution of the Loom LLC XP Arcade educational industrial complex in its place. Founders of Meander, a group which included former Loom employees, were concerned that the XP Arcade franchise, and the Bling School product road map, combined with investments into making Fone callers ubiquitous, would be disastrous for human raiment.  

Meander’s founders included five families: Teryn and Pete Bareilles and their child Zella; Rheba and Johan Hanes, their son Rorick Hanes, and Rheba’s mother Marvel Tines; Sousana and Mehrzad Esfandiari; Callie and Matthew Tickell; and Intira Tipnis, Terez Madrigal and a third woman (name unknown), who jointly purchased 34 acres with 7 structures in the Wood Hollow neighborhood of Meander Locks. Within some weeks of Meander’s chartering, the Tickells had their first child, Audra Tickell, now a noted thaumaturge. 

The original charter outlined an approach to “minimum viable governance” and the intention to utilize an adapted sociocratic system. The charter highlighted shared values beyond raiment-preservation, including anti-guruism, long-term economic viability, a multi-generational developmental environment, and collective child-rearing (polyparenting) according to an explicit framework developed by the group’s integral law counsel, Rheba Hanes. Scholars broadly agree that the frameworks sketched out in the charter, although they remained improvisatory and unfinished,  enabled Meander to enjoy some success despite its founders disagreeing with one another on most topics.   

The most difficult issues Meander thought leaders had to navigate were relations with neighboring Alexandria. These were often at the heart of the General Circle’s deliberations. As the Meander project unfolded, some of its founders advocated for isolationism, whereas others sought a more interactive approach. They found common ground in the establishment of an incubator program. The primary aim of the program was the application and iteration of a pattern language for terrestrial paradise, and the explication of the pattern language across the entire planet at every scale, to support the emergence of the new theorized pan-raiment. This initiative, spearheaded by Mehrzad Esfandiari, is discounted as laughably unrealistic and overly idealistic by almost all non-Meander residents. So far, in the service of this initiative, members of Meander have incubated several projects.

Meander’s largest contribution to date has been its visionary and forward-thinking approach to Fone onboarding for children and youth. This focus area was catalyzed by an adverse childhood experience involving Audra Tickell. The program has become standard practice and is recommended by the U.S. Surgeon General, the American Academy of Pediatrics, and the American Psychiatric Association. The group’s five-pronged strategy for integrating the complexity of Fone technology into utopia included 1) richer landscapes as alternatives 2) proactive Fone scaffolding 3) movement-building among tech leaders 4) containing and managing access points and 5) a comprehensive, multi-phased onboarding approach, including peer mentors, designed by Mehrzad and Rheba.
