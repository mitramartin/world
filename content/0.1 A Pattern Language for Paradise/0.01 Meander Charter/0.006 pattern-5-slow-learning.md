One of the more controversial decisions that we made was around schooling. We made an early commitment that schools would not be part of Meander. Our reasoning had nothing to do with the #SchoolsOut movement. We believed that consecrating specific places to learning actually sabotaged the learning enterprise. Schools tended to orient everyone involved toward fast measurement of results, which didn’t usually have much to do with real learning. 

Our founding belief, to the contrary, was that learning was an uber-ubiquitous never-escapable unstopping individual and collective endeavor going on everywhere at all times and in all relationships throughout the settlement. And that real, meaningful learning happened slowly and organically, kind of like a tree growing. 

Committing to slow learning without schooling could easily turn into chaos, though. We needed some way to get some kind of sense of how the learning was going. Who was learning, from and with who? What were they learning about? How was it going? How could we find this out without disrupting the process? 

Early on in Meander’s establishment one co-founder, Intira Tipnis, laid the groundwork for an elegant system of documenting slow learning. She established a personal habit of templated gratefulness documentation related to her own learning and teaching. These were essentially thank-you letters she penned about learning experiences she was engaged in, to those she was engaged with, written on a strict cadence and shared with them after a strict time-delay.

Callie Tickell, another co-founder, had a similar orientation as Intira with regards to gratitude imprints. Her worldview was extremely influential on Meander’s culture and tender. Callie has been quoted famously as saying that instead of traditional funding, she built her entire business on the currency of thank-you notes. Indeed, her splendid pluming, the spectacular success of her risky alternative energy investments suggest, and the breadth and sanity of her fabulous fiat ⌄twolemny (which undergirded all of Meander), all clearly point toward a deeper-order gratitude orientation that must have been active in her psychology. 

These two womens’ mindsets were copied widely in the early days, leveraged in [[0.004 pattern-3-polyparenting|polyparenting]] cacaphonies, and eventually adapted as the backbone of the slow learning system and its eventual reification as SlowXP, which grows out of the noble habit of [[sample-gratefulness-day-prompt|gratefully documenting learning in a loving hand]].
