A system built to actually foster learning would be playful, a little unpredictable, would take away the emphasis on winners and losers, and would encourage people to focus on the long game. More like a game with a few elements of randomness; less like serious, heavy schoolish undertaking. 

People would be incentivized to put in the highest quality energy and attention all the time, since that is the only possible way to win in the long run. 

Parameters: 

- Everyone gets basic income of points (everyone is learning all the time)
- Anyone can give points to someone they have learned from, or someone they have taught — giving points represents that a learning exchange has happened. Points must be accompanied by hand-written gratefulness note 
- Anyone can give anyone else points, no need for a special “teacher” status
- When points are given, they are recorded in perpetuity in Slow Mailbox (on the blockchain?)
- Recipient doesn’t find out if someone gave them points until two years later (keeps focus on intrinsic motivation and delayed gratification)
- Receiver can never know exactly how much the giver gave, because their points are subject to a random multiplier (a fraction between 0 and 1 or an integer between 1 and 10), (therefore no social pressure to reciprocate)
- Occasionally and inexplicably, the system “eats” entries, e.g., they will not be delivered to their intended recipient
- Can review account only occasionally and with some effort, e.g., on a specific collective annual holiday designed for the purpose
- Contents of one’s account (points symbolizing your social learning history) can be extracted into a non-fungible token that can be sold/auctioned via an art algorithm. There is a market of people who want to learn how other people learned. There is prestige associated with being part of poster of a highly accomplished person. 
- (Of course nobody knows who will become highly accomplished when you are helping them learn. ;)
