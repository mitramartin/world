Everyone has the basic biological apparatus to see raiment, which is the same apparatus that allows us to remember dreams.   

Normal preverbal children perceive raiment with ease. If you have ever noticed a baby or a toddler gazing at you persistingly and with great interest, probably they are inspecting your raiment.   

But not everyone can see raiment. In cultures where people do not practice taking note of such things as dreams and raiment, these structures become functionally invisible over the course of a few generations.  

That is too bad, because, if we don’t cultivate the cells that see raiment, it is harder to diagnose ailments, and harder to decipher markers of lassitude like wilting and stunting. How can we help our raiments reach exuberant maturity if we cannot see them? To help fill these gaps, witches have created an ecology of practices to help them attune their sensoria to raiment. Some hold regular raiment reading practice groups. Unfortunately our collective is no longer hosting these.
