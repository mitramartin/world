title: 0.002 When humanity’s raiment problems began   
---    
Humanity’s raiment problems began about two thousand years ago with the first Alexandrias. They started under the reign of a fool named Alexander, who rushed from place to place and wove a world in his name. In his brief life he established over five hundred Alexandrias, and then died suddenly, trampled by his horse. [^1] To this day, an Alexandria is a settlement set up for the glory of one or two men, at the cost of all raiment. The polises assert a dominating beefcake orderliness at the cost of the real order that raiment arranges. They are, by name, places of ill raiment. 

In conformance with leadership, Alexandrine high society thinks of raiment at best as a distracting folk tale, at worst as a powder-keg packed with subversive and potentially explosive ramifications. Most adopt a protectively derisive and permanently eye-rolly stance.

[^1]:Some maintain that this never happened; that he never did die; that he agreed to be secretly buried alive under one of his Alexandrias in a nine-storied tomb called Inferno, great underground death city, which was accessed secretly and variously by cronies, witches, spiders, admen, and spelunkers over the years, each with different motive and which finally in our time has solidified into a known place of tourism and industry. That, as the name of his frozen coffin was passed from one generation’s lips to the next’s ears, in time it slowly changed to what we know it as today: Land of [[0.006 what-is-fone|Fone]], our own cold holy underground complex, our storehouse for the undead. This myth, probably apocryphal, contradicts other accounts of the mind-made origins of Fone.
