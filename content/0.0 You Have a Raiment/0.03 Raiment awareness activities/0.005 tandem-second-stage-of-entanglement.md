Multiple [[0.004 tanda-first-stage-of-entanglement|tandas]] with the same partner that take place across a variety of spaces and times naturally form a tandem. Tandems are on average about 100x as strong as a tandas, which is amazing, and they are difficult, maybe impossible, to disrupt, although they can go into hibernation for years. 

Because of the profound entanglement in a tandem (usually, the involved tendres actually become knitted into one another in intricately interconnected non-visible braids), each individual raiment benefits from a huge influx of complementary nutrients from the other, and they confer on one another a high degree of structural integrity. The unification endures across space and time, with tendre plaits continuing to send and receive information parcels and other minerals via breezes, fiats, or pollinating insects.   

Researchers who study raiment in cross-cultural settings have demonstrated that tandems occur even in cultures that deny raiment, and go by the term “marriage,” with a few important distinctions. 

- Tandems are non-excluding, but marriages often are exclusive
- Marriages seem to require mouth-to-mouth kissing, but tandems do not
- Tandems are completely non-bureaucratic, but marriages include extensive paperwork

In less marriage-centric cultures, tandems are also witnessed in the form of identity crushes, frenemies, work spouses, or any sort of “other significant other” arrangement.
