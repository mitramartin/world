---
title: Raiment preventative care
---    

A raiment is a complex ecosystem, not unlike the gut. Basic health care is less about sanitation than creating a sufficiently alkaline environment that allows the flora to thrive, so they enable bonding between tendres.   

For this to happen, a raiment requires nightly dreaming and daily meandering exposure to fresh, plant-ventilated air. Beyond basic nutrition, it needs the experience of convivial feasting, e.g., sharing food, drink and conversation with others, two or three times weekly. These activities provide it with the minerals its flora need to support the raiment’s structural integrity and entanglement functions. 

Researchers have discovered a surprising and inexplicable correlation between a raiment’s agility and durability, and the skilling of the hand. Children who carry out more varied and complex activities with their hands tend to reach adulthood with a more robust raiment. (cf. M. Montessori.)
