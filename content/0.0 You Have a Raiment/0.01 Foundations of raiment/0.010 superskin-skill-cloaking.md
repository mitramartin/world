---
title: Superskin skill- Cloaking
---    

The hallmark of a great raiment is a continuous protective superskin: a cloak. Even if your raiment is robust, well-nourished, and exuberant, it takes work to cloak, and like everything else, it takes practice! 

Summoning your filaments to cloak and maintaining it even for a few minutes actually requires a nontrivial expenditure of energy. It takes electrical energy to pull the filaments down into a cascade and to lightly bond the outermost filaments to one another; and it takes radiant energy to hold the boundary, keeping unwanted substances (clutter, noise, temptation, bugs) out of it. Building this capacity is a bit like building muscle. 

Artifacts that frame activities and visually bound space can help the developing raiment learn how to enclose itself. For instance, trays, placemats, small carpets or floor mats all reinforce and support cloaking.

Cultural norms that allow [completion of tasks without interruption](https://mitramartin.substack.com/p/your-childs-attention-is-sacred) are very helpful for training the activation muscles, too. If a child attempts to cloak repeatedly but is interrupted each time, she may become frustrated and stop attempting. Although her activation muscles are still there, her capacity to create her own enclosure becomes atrophied. 

Over time, with more and more cloaking practice, a person’s superskin becomes stronger. That is to say, it can stay activated for longer and longer periods of time. A strong cloaking is thin but impermeable to outer influences, even in fragmenting and polluted circumstances.
