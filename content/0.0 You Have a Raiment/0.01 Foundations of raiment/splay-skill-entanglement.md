#### Entanglement

Tendres are exquisitely sensitive. They are continuously exploring the space around the person and sniffing it carefully, even during sleep. They are also continuously signaling to nearby tendres by emitting a subtle catalytic pheromone-like substance. 

Here is how entanglement occurs. (This whole sequence can happen almost instantaneously, but for purpose of instruction we break it into steps here.)

**Approach**. Let’s say you and another person are in the same general vicinity. Under normal circumstances, one or more of your tendres will follow the scent of the other person’s tendres, and that person’s tendres will do the same. 

**Touch**. Your tendres will approach one another and assuming you each find the others’ scent suitable your tendres will touch. This is the beginning of acquaintanceship, whether or not you’ve actually exchanged words. Some say that tendre pheromones are actually silent words. 

**Facing**. A tendre touch could be the start of everything! Or, it could be nothing. Numinous phenomena may ensue, or not, depending on a process we now know as facing. Facing is an encounter that involves a coherent trinity of face, voice, and name, ideally (but not always) in the witness of the sun. This is why dysmorphic disorders make entanglement so difficult: if one is unaligned with their voice, face, or name they cannot participate in a facing.

**Interlace**. Once the facing has happened, entanglement usually proceeds apace. The tendres acclimate to one anothers’ electricity, and there ensues a period of interlacing. There the tendres will begin to intertwine, then release and retreat, for quite some time, rather a shy kind of flirtation. Avoidance and reserve is as important to this part of the process as play and seeking. 

**Unify**. Over time and repeated intertwinings and retreats, the pair of tendres will slowly become a single functional unit. Not that they will fuse together, but rather their movements will become so responsive to one another that they will form part of a single system. 

Entanglement is always mutual, reciprocal, complementary, and consensual. In the past, it was said that entanglement was also always embodied; however, it has been recently demonstrated that entanglements are in fact possible across remote distances between the tendres of bodies that have never encountered one another in physical space, which suggests that their Facing likely happened in dream or past life (for instance).

When you entangle with another, the exchange of nutrients between tendres invigorates them, and so does the exercise. As a result, over time and with much practice, each entangled tendre slowly grows, thickens, and becomes more elastic, bringing more structural integrity to the entangled unit.
#### Chunking

Chunking essentially refers to the skill of managing multiple simultaneous entanglements. This entails the capacity to consciously track how many active entanglements one has; and, to begin, pause, or close entanglements to ensure an optimal volume. 

It also involves learning to direct the same tendre or group of tendres toward the same companion repeatedly over time, so they can more easily acclimate to one another and discover their dance. 
#### Orchestrating

Tendre entanglements can be simple or complex. More complex entanglements means partnerships are forged across more tendres, or groups of tendres. And the inter-tendre connections themselves have richer patterning, e.g., multiple different simultaneous rhythms and intensities of approach/retreat and pausing.
### Pluming

A happy, healthy raiment under normal circumstances will blossom. A stalk will erupt from the center of the nimbus, grow, and blossom with a brightly colored richly ornamented flower spike: a Plume! At first these were seen as merely decorative but, as you will learn in future chapters, they actually have important personal and societal functions. In brief, plumes are used mostly in the context of attracting valuable and expansive inter-species partnerships.
