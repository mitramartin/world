---
title: Audra’s Capstone for her Sixteenth
author: Audra Tickell
---
My parents founded Meander in search of their own raiment. They said it was for us kids, but I know it was really for them. The others did too. Back then, you know, nobody could find their raiment. Except of course Sousana. 

It must have felt like a huge win when Sousana finally committed to the project. After months of wavering. Thaumaturges were super rare then, and lots of them had, let’s just say, problematic personalities. My folks couldn’t have done it without Sousana, legit. They needed someone who could see raiment. Not to mention that Sousana brought a pretty badass plus one: Mehrzad was now part of utopia too.   

(Did you know that the only reason Sousana came was because the oracle? That’s what she told me. She was totally on the fence, and when she asked she got a definitive mandate. Amazeballs. If she hadn’t’ve come, no way I’d be sitting here writing this.) 

Sousana told me the story so many times, when I was little. She and Mehrzad were the first to scope out the land. Over the course of a year, each of the investors left their respective Alexandrias, one couple at a time, and moved into their quarters. Mom and Dad took up residence in one of the five rambly farmhouses across the acreage, next to the giant pole barn, which would ultimately become the great house. Rheba and Johan settled into the yellow one, along with Marvel, Rheba’s mom. Teryn and Kai came in their RV, last but with huge energy, and built a couple of ADUs within a couple months of arriving. Sousana and Mehrzad lived in the two side-by-side pop-up yurts. One of them is still there. We use it for ceremony. 

Sousana used to tell me how odd it was, the way the founders came to the Meander project. They were all so full of hope and ferocity and big tech wealth and grand writings. No one but her could see how pathetic they actually were, how they dragged their withered and blighted raiments behind them. 

It’s hard to imagine that now. I mean, they are all elders now, and their raiment is super potent. So you could say the experiment worked. The just plain gorgeousness of Meander and its whole imperfect paradise vibe was healing for real. Dad told me how really lost he was when I was born, and how his first three or four tandems helped mend the bald places in his upper folds and bring spring back into his cloaking. Out of everyone, my mom’s raiment was in the best shape, but how would she have known? She told me about how she wept when she saw her Plume for the first time after a Blue Moon Cypher one morning. It’s like she didn’t really know it was real, even though it had gifted us all with enough acreage of happy fiat to live here for generations.   

They were all super lucky. I’m super lucky. The oracle did me a solid. And I know they all did their best and it’s hard to fault them for what happened. If you had read the literature back then, if you were even barely exposed to counterculture, it would have been easy enough to work out that Alexandria’s dynamics were keeping human raiment in a sad awful sickly state. But most people there didn’t care or have enough energy to do anything about it. My parent’s generation took action to change it, for me, for us. 

No, I don’t blame them, not even my dad. What happened was a mistake. I fell, I sure did. But I survived, and I think I’m stronger for it, and in a way looking back now it’s a good thing that it happened that way because it helped me be able to help the kids who fell later. The kids from Alexandria.
