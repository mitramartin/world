Dear Zella, 

You asked me one time [[what-is-raiment|what raiment is]]. 

It is a part of your body that is very important that many adults have forgotten about. (You may still be able to see parts of it.)  

It is actually part of your head, and looks a bit like your hair. In fact you may have seen it mixed into your hair. It has really really thin extremely long tentacles, like a jellyfish or a sea anemone has. We call those long tentacle-like parts “tendres,” which is pronounced “tenders.” 

Like your brain, it is superintelligent and can do so many things. It is hard to explain all that it can do! 

One of the most important things it can do is make a little room around you, like a bubble or a very long umbrella. Inside, it feels calm, and when you are in it, it is easy to focus on what you’re doing. If you ever got really into making something, or drawing something, or fixing something, or solving something, or writing something, it was probably because your raiment’s tendres billowed around you like that. 

Another super important thing it does is making friends. The making of friends is actually literally something your raiment makes, by how your tendres tangle with someone else’s. Tendres are very good at telling who will be a good friend! If you have a really great friend, especially one you do projects and make things with, then probably your tendres and theirs are entangled, which is very fun and healthy (and good for your raiment.)   

The third thing that your raiment does is extremely important and a little less common. It helps you get inspirations that are specifically and only made for you. Your most sensitive and thinnest and longest tendres at the very top of your head are like a long lightning rod. They can connect to wise people in other places and even from other times, like your own ancestors, or even your future self. They can plug you into crucial information that is inside of dreams or held by oracles, or instruments from long ago that play forgotten melodies that were intended for your life. 

So, raiment is very helpful and important. It can be thick and plush, or angel hair fine, or more sparse like a beaded curtain. But it is strong and muscular and wise and it gets stronger as you grow especially if you take care of it. You take care of it mostly by feasting which means eating by candlelight with your family, and dreaming deeply, and walking in sunshine, and by using your hands in many different ways.  

The only thing you need to know is that there are some places that are full of teeth that bite and chew your raiment right off your head. Without a raiment it is harder to know what to do, harder to focus on what you’re doing, and harder to have the right helpful friends to make stuff with. 

These raiment-eating places aren’t in themselves bad, but they just are set up to shred human raiment. Kind of like how food processors aren’t in themselves bad, they are just made up of little knives moving quickly set up to shred any food you put near them. 

You don’t want your raiment to be food for someone or something else. So I want to teach you all you need to know to keep it whole and luminous and mischievous. That’s what this is about. 

People with broken or shorn raiments are sick. We call them Amused. Amused in a hard, closed-up kind of way, not a soft funny way. Think of that mean kind of laughing someone does, that makes them more alone, and their eyes are hard. Since their raiment has been cut, they don’t know what to do, how to focus on what is happening, or who can help them. So they get isolated and lonely, and ultimately this causes the last resort of connection, which is laughing at other people’s hurt.

I love you and I am working on building a place that is good for your raiment. That's why I have been far away for a long time. It is a lot of work. But we are very well entangled. 

Numinous tidings, 
Sousana
